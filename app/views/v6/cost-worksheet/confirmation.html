What to do next

Download your spending worksheet (PDF, 15KB) to share with the other parent. You can also download a spreadsheet (CSV, 15KB) to use for your records.

If you want to split costs equally

If you both want to spend the same amount, you should each fill out the spending worksheet, or complete it using the combined amounts that you currently spend. Then, divide the total costs in half.

For example, if Parent 1 usually spends £50 each week and Parent 2 spends £70 for a total of £120, they would agree to spend £60 each week. They would send each other money as needed to make sure isn't spending too much.

If you want to split costs based on care arrangements

If you want to spends amounts based on how much day-to-day care you have, you should each fill out the spending worksheet, or complete it using the combined amounts that you currently spend.

Then, divide the total costs by 7. This will be the amount, on average, that you would spend for each day your child is in your care.

For example, if both parents together spend about £105 each week, they spend about £15 for each day. If their child stays with Parent 1 on the weekends (for about £30), Parent 2 spends about £75 for the other 5 days. Parent 1 may agree to pay Parent 2 £25 each week to match their spending.

You can also use the Child Maintenance Calculator to work out payments. It considers income, care arrangements and other children.

If you want to split costs based on care arrangements

You may want to change your financial arrangment so that the parent with a larger income spends more for their child.

For example, if Parent 1 has most of the day-to-day care but their income is half of Parent 2's income, Parent 2 may agree to send payments for 75% of their child's needs. This means that for every £100 Parent 1 spends on their child, Parent 2 sends £75.

You can also use the Child Maintenance Calculator to work out payments. It considers income, care arrangements and other children.
